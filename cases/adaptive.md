## Summary

Adaptive replaces the clerical work, spreadsheets and point tools around a contractor's general ledger, not the ledger itself [1][2]. Its AI agents read bills, code them to job and cost code, chase lien waivers and field updates by voice, text or email, and send human-approved entries to QuickBooks, Sage Intacct, Foundation or Acumatica [1][2]. It grew from about 10 paying customers in January 2023 to 750+ construction companies with $5M to $1B in revenue, 40+ accounting firms and $57M raised by September 2026, with revenue undisclosed [3][1]. The lesson: start below the construction ERP's floor with a one-sided overlay on the ledger small firms already use, then climb, but treat replacing the ledger as a separate bet that Adaptive has not yet made [2].

## The legacy it attacks

Construction accounting runs on two tiers of legacy. Small builders keep books in QuickBooks, which Adaptive's founders learned in order to run such clients' books [4]. Project finance lives in email, Excel and file sharing [5]. Larger contractors run construction ERPs. Sage 300 CRE began as Timberline in 1971 and passed to Sage in 2003 [6]. Foundation has sold since 1985 and says it has helped more than 43,000 construction professionals [7]. Trimble paid $1.2B for Viewpoint in 2018, when Viewpoint had about 8,000 customers and 46% of the ENR 400, Engineering News-Record's largest contractors [8]. In JBKnowledge's 2020 survey, Sage (21%), Viewpoint (17%), QuickBooks (7%) and Foundation (4%) ranked among the most-used accounting systems [9]. Controllers, accounts payable (AP) clerks, office managers and outside bookkeepers run them day to day (inferred).

They stuck for five reasons.

- **Reporting duty.** Sureties treat the work-in-progress (WIP) schedule as the core of a bonding file and prefer GAAP statements with cost-to-complete revenue recognition [10]. A new ledger puts that file at risk (inferred).
- **Failed replacements.** In the UK, Kier's Oracle ERP grew from a £30m budget to £90m [11]. Balfour Beatty wrote down £21m and Interserve £17m on curtailed or abandoned rollouts [11].
- **Tolerated tech debt.** Sage 300 CRE still runs on a Pervasive/Actian database, and Sage warns that a dropped VPN can lock files and damage data [12]. Sage has announced no end of life [6].
- **People as middleware.** 51% of surveyed firms relied on spreadsheets for accounting or ERP workflows [9]. When apps did not integrate, 49% moved data by hand [9]. Only 5% said all their apps integrated [9].
- **Skills.** Over 300,000 accountants left the US profession between 2019 and 2022 [13]. Staff who know the current system are hard to replace and slow to retrain (inferred).

No public source shows long lock-in contracts; data, reports and skills do the holding (inferred).

## Origin

Matt Calvano (CEO) worked in investment banking and private equity and holds a Stanford MBA [14]. Henry Bradlow (CTO) developed algorithms for rocket systems at SpaceX, and Francisco Enriquez was the third co-founder [4]. None came from construction [2]. They started in summer 2021 [15] as Adaptive Real Estate, Inc. [16], with a zoning tool for developers and land work for Austin homebuilders [2][4]. Calls to contractors for cost estimates exposed a different pain: owners could not see project profit until the end, double-paid subs and forgot to bill change orders [2]. They pivoted, rented an Airbnb and ran a bookkeeping firm for local builders for a couple of months, charging monthly and learning QuickBooks [2][4]. A $750K pre-seed closed in August 2021 [16].

Why then. Invoice reading worked with optical character recognition (OCR) and machine learning by 2022 [4]. By 2024 Adaptive said it had built generative AI algorithms for construction bookkeeping [5]. A startup could offer fee-free bill pay [17] and, through a bank partner, issue cards [18]. Intuit's suite for mid-market, project-based firms arrived only in September 2024 [19], which left room for a layer above QuickBooks (inferred).

## First wedge

The first job was accounts payable, end to end. The February 2022 site offered: forward, text or scan bills; AI-filled fields mapped to accounting codes; automatic requests to subs for missing details; approval rules; payment by check, transfer or card; and links to existing accounting and project management software. It also listed budgets, corporate cards and timesheets [20]. By December 2022 bill pay carried $0 transaction fees [17]. The target was the small general contractor (GC) without accounting staff [4], narrowed by March 2023 to builders and remodelers [21].

Why that job. AP is daily, painful and countable in hours, so value shows within weeks (inferred). No ledger migration was needed, because Adaptive synced with QuickBooks [22], which it says has 8 million users [23]. AP also sits next to money movement, which fit the 2022 plan for a one-stop shop of financial products, from insurance to working capital [4].

**Sold.** Sales ran through an early-access program [17], led by the founders (inferred). About 10 beta users had converted to paying by January 2023, before the public launch [3]. **Priced.** In March 2023 the pricing page listed no price, only a promise to cost less than what builders pay for dumpsters [27]. ## What made it hard

- **Outsiders.** The founders bought credibility by keeping builders' books [2] and hired a sales lead with a builder-facing background [3].
- **Resistant buyers.** On a 2024 podcast, Adaptive's sales lead described builders with software fatigue [3]. A 2023 profile described invoices, draws, banks and trade partners that change with every job and every state [26].
- **Small tickets.** List prices rose from $300 a month (Mar 2024) [28] to $575 for firms up to $10M (Jun 2025) [41] and $599 for firms up to $5M (Dec 2025) [42], with no transaction fees [43]. At the $599 entry price, 750 customers would yield about $5.4M ARR. That is a reference point, not an estimate, since larger firms pay custom prices (inferred). In 2026 the public price list gave way to fees scoped by revenue band and agent count [43].
- **Heavier delivery upmarket.** Setup went from about 30 minutes [22] to agents configured by Adaptive engineers and live in 30 to 90 days [43]. That looks more like services than self-serve software (inferred).
- **Thin ERP links.** QuickBooks Desktop syncs most records both ways, on a schedule [44]. Sage Intacct receives transactions one way, on manual push, with no edits pulled back [45]. Foundation, Sage 300 CRE, Vista and Spectrum connect by CSV import and export [46], and Adams-Bickel uses Foundation-ready exports [36]. Calvano's next milestone is more ERP integrations [2]. Agave's investor Accel says it has two-way links into 14+ ERP and project-management systems [47]. Field Materials posts into nine accounting systems for firms such as Swinerton [48].
- **Errors.** Capterra shows 63 reviews averaging 5.0, yet reviewers cite misread invoices, QuickBooks Desktop sync bugs and vendor-credit problems [49].
- **Incumbent response.** Intuit added Enterprise Suite (September 2024) [19] and a construction edition with a Project Management Agent (February 2026) [50]. Foundation added AI invoice reading to its A/P automation [51]. Procore announced Procore Pay with Goldman Sachs in November 2022 [52]. Trimble bought Flashtract [53].
- **Controls.** Money moves through partners: cards are issued by First Internet Bank of Indiana with capital from CapitalOS [18]. Every agent action needs human approval before it reaches the ledger [1], and WIP numbers feed surety files [10].

## Strategy

Adaptive is an overlay with traits of an AI-run service. It owns no ledger and sells against migration [43]. We found no acquisitions. In Calvano's words, "We're not replacing anyone's ERP, we sit on top of it" [2].

The route from wedge toward the core has five steps.

1. **AP and bill pay on QuickBooks**, 2022 to 2023 [20][17].
2. **Sub-ledger breadth.** Budgets, draws, vendor compliance, lien waivers and purchase orders by 2024 [22][54], then its own charge card in May 2025 [18], after the 2022 site had already pitched corporate cards [20].
3. **A channel.** A program for accounting firms launched in May 2025, live with 6 of the top 100 firms [55]. Adaptive claims a firm can serve 3 to 5 times more clients per staffer [56].
4. **Upmarket.** A Procore integration in October 2025 [32], then Sage Intacct, Foundation and Acumatica by September 2026 [1].
5. **Agents**, June 2026: WIP and forecasting, field coordination, AP, change orders, billings, card and bank reconciliation, and lien-waiver and insurance tracking [57]. Agents call or text superintendents for job facts [57][2].

The pitch changed along the way. In 2022 the goal was a one-stop shop for financial products [4]. In 2024 it was an all-in-one back office including accounting and payroll [15], plus a payments ecosystem linking developers, GCs and subs [29]. In 2026 it is an agent layer that leaves the ledger in place [1]. Tidemark calls it an example of a System of Action, software that acts on data and triggers work rather than only storing it [1][58].

How far it has got: Adaptive has replaced hours and point tools, not ledgers. Its cases cite an avoided AP hire [36], lien waivers folded in from a separate tool [37] and an avoided bookkeeper [38]. The books stay in QuickBooks, Sage or Foundation [43]. Payroll, a 2024 goal, is absent from the 2026 agent list [59].

**Contrast with Briq.** Ex-Procore executive Bassem Hamdy and Ron Goldshmidt founded Briq in 2018 [60] (Nemetschek says 2017 [62]). Its first plan, a construction data cloud, failed because the industry's decades-old software had no APIs [61]. It pivoted to robotic process automation (RPA) bots that type into legacy systems and sold top-down to CFOs [61]. It reached about $1.5M ARR in 2020 and eight-figure revenue later [61], with integrations from QuickBooks to Vista [62]. Hamdy says headcount peaked at 300, then fell to 100 [61]; TechCrunch counted 138 at the end of 2023 [60]. Its January 2024 extension came at a flat $150M valuation [60]. Adaptive started smaller, used APIs where they existed and CSV files elsewhere [46], and ran leaner: 29 staff at 280 customers [5][29].

**Contrast with Flashtract.** Flashtract launched in Atlanta in January 2020 (Trimble dates it to 2019) to move GC-to-sub pay applications, lien waivers and compliance documents onto one platform [63][53]. It raised $15M led by Addition and Shine Capital in January 2022 [63] and integrated with Sage 300, Procore and Vista [64]. Its value depended on GCs and subs both adopting it (inferred). Its founder said its best customers used Trimble's ERP [65]. Trimble bought it in May 2024 on undisclosed terms, took its 24 staff and renamed it Trimble Pay, first integrated with Vista [65][53]. Adaptive asks only its customer's own team to log in [35] and spans 10+ ledgers [1].

## What others can copy

1. **Do the job by hand first.** Adaptive ran a paid bookkeeping service before it built software [2][4]. Test: do customers pay for manual delivery within 60 days?
2. **Enter below the vertical ERP's floor.** Adaptive charged $300 a month on top of QuickBooks [28]. Test: is the entry price under 10% of the hire it removes, and is setup shorter than the sale?
3. **Make adoption one-sided.** Reach field staff by voice, text or email and vendors by automatic requests, not a portal [2][20]. Flashtract needed both sides (inferred). Test: what share of workflow steps finish with zero new external accounts?
4. **Cap exposure to any one ledger.** Flashtract's best customers sat on one vendor's ERP, and that vendor bought it [65]. Test: no single GL above, say, 50% of ARR (inferred threshold).
5. **Recruit the accountants.** Adaptive works with 40+ accounting firms [1]. Test: compare retention of partner-sourced and direct accounts.
6. **Gate the ledger with a human, and staff to revenue.** Adaptive's agents never post without approval [1], and Briq grew to 300 staff before cutting to 100, by its CEO's account [61]. Test: track approval-without-edit rates and revenue per employee.

## Open questions

- Revenue, gross margin and retention are undisclosed. The only growth rates come from a sponsored newsletter [35].
- Adaptive claimed more than 1,000 customers in February 2026 [33] and about 1,000 businesses and 50 accounting firms in a 2026 conference bio that still cited $26M raised [14], then 750+ and 40+ in September [1]. Was that churn of month-to-month accounts, pruning, or a new definition?
- Can CSV links into Foundation, Vista and Sage 300 CRE [46] hold mid-market accounts against rivals with native two-way links [47]?
- Can engineer-configured agents [43] keep software margins as the customer count grows (inferred risk)?
- Will Intuit's construction edition [50] or Foundation's AP automation [51] absorb the wedge from inside the ledger?
- Will Adaptive ever own the ledger, or stay a layer that the GL vendor can copy?
- Payments carry no transaction fees [43]. Where will fintech revenue come from, if anywhere?
