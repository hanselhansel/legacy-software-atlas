# Agriculture and commodity trading

Slug: `agriculture-commodity`

## Systems

- Grain and ag-retail accounting ERPs for elevators and cooperatives: AGRIS (Greenstone/Cultura, PC 1984, Windows 2000-03), Agvance (SSI, 1981 origin, Windows mid-1990s), AgTrax/TraxView (1996), Merchant Ag (Ever.Ag/EFC), AgVision, Primetics, AMIC, CORETech (Cultura/Constellation portfolio)
- Commodity trading and risk management (CTRM) for traders and processors: ION Openlink Endur, ION TriplePoint (Triple Point Technology, 1993), ION Agtech, ION Allegro, Softmar (chartering), iRely i21 (successor to older iRely origination/grain products)
- Large-enterprise ERP estates at global merchants (e.g. ADM worldwide ERP programme), often with SAP Commodity Management
- Workarounds around the core: spreadsheets for analytics and non-physical trades, email/paper for post-trade execution, paper books at palm-fruit dealers

## Legacy signs (first pass)

- **S1 pre-cloud core**: met. Market-leading grain accounting systems were built before 2005 as on-prem PC/Windows client-server software. AGRIS shipped on PC in 1984, and its Windows version came in 2000-2003. SSI was founded in 1981 and wrote its Windows Agvance suite in the mid-1990s (cloud Agvance SKY only arrived in 2016). Triple Point's CTRM dates from 1993. No mainframe or IBM i grain system was confirmed, so S1 rests on on-prem client-server. ([source](https://greenstonesystems.com/agris/))
- **S2 slow to replace**: met. ADM's FY2016 10-K says its worldwide ERP rollout will 'occur in phases over the next several years'. Its FY2024 10-K still says the upgrades, including changes to legacy systems, will take 'phases over the next several years', and that no ERP deployments happened in 2024. That is 8+ years of an unfinished replacement at a top grain merchant. Separately, Adaptive's case study for a global agribusiness CTRM describes a multi-phase build delivered by a large distributed team 'over an extended period of time', with logistics and invoicing due in a later year. FY2016 10-K: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000007084/000000708417000008/adm-20161231x10k.htm ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/7084/000000708425000011/adm-20241231.htm))
- **S3 workarounds**: met. Consensys's Covantis case study says more than 275 million emails a year are exchanged to process about 11,000 ocean grain shipments, and it targets a 90% cut in rekeyed entries. An Eka survey of 50 commodity executives (agriculture, chemicals, oil, gas, power) found 66% use spreadsheets for analysis and 12% use CTRM (https://quoreka.com/news/new-eka-survey-finds-two-out-of-three-commodities-companies-using-spreadsheets-for-analysis). Adaptive's agri CTRM case study plans to decommission 'an old, Excel-based solution' for non-physical trades (https://weareadaptive.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Adaptive-Case-Study-Agricultural-commodities-trading-risk-management-and-logistics-CTRM.pdf). An MPOB journal paper notes that Malaysian palm-fruit dealers keep records in books or on paper (https://palmoilis.mpob.gov.my/publications/OPIEJ/opiejv23n1-parthiban.pdf). ([source](https://consensys.io/blockchain-use-cases/global-trade-and-commerce/covantis))
- **S4 shrinking skills**: met. Weak evidence, and not about COBOL or RPG. A specialist E/CTRM recruiter says there is 'a small pool of people who actually know the systems', and that most specialists who have run a live migration are not on the open market. Endur is the named example. Capco's Openlink Endur paper says the skills needed are 'not generally available within either the vendor or client resources' (https://www.capco.com/-/media/CapcoMedia/Subfolder-Unsorted/Capco-2/PDFs/Implementation_Pitfalls_Openlink_Endur.ashx). Both sources lean toward energy. I found no source showing COBOL or RPG skills scarcity for grain accounting systems (no IBM i grain product confirmed). Treat this as met only for CTRM product skills. Downgrade to false if strict COBOL/RPG evidence is required. ([source](https://cititec.com/trading-risk-management-systems-talent/))

## Segments

Four tiers, all evidenced. (1) SMB and mid-market: US/Canadian country elevators, farmer cooperatives and ag retailers run on-prem grain and ag-retail ERPs. Agvance alone reports 3,800+ ag retail locations, and AGRIS scales 'from one to hundreds of locations' (greenstonesystems.com/agris; agvance.net/about-ssi). GTA membership ranges from regional family businesses to national and international traders (graintrade.org.au letter, May 2025). (2) Mid-market and enterprise traders and processors use CTRM. ION says 300+ agricultural organisations use its products, and Triple Point had 350+ customers at its 2013 sale. (3) Enterprise: global merchants run multi-year ERP and legacy-system replacements, e.g. ADM's worldwide ERP rollout ran from its FY2016 10-K to its FY2024 10-K, and six majors founded Covantis to replace email/paper post-trade execution. (4) Government and state agencies (PH NFA/BPI licensing, VN MOIT rice export certification, MY MPOB licensing, IN e-NAM) appear here as registers and regulators. No source I opened showed which legacy software these agencies run.

## Reasons buyers stay

- Grain accounting systems embed scale-ticket, contract, settlement, warehouse-receipt and patronage logic tied to local regulation (e.g. USWA licensing, farmer tax exemptions), so replacement means rebuilding compliance-critical workflows
- Global merchants' ERP/CTRM replacements run for years in phases (ADM: FY2016 to FY2024 and still not finished), with material control risk (ADM's FY2024 10-K reports a material weakness in internal control and no ERP deployments that year)
- Vendors keep modernising in place (AGRIS added MS SQL in 2014-16, Agvance added the SKY cloud version in 2016), which lowers the pressure to switch
- Consolidated vendor ownership (Constellation/Volaris via Cultura; ION across Openlink, TriplePoint, Allegro and Agtech) keeps products supported long-term
- Integration overlays (e.g. Bushel integrates with existing grain accounting systems instead of replacing them) extend the life of the legacy core
- Scarce specialist implementation skills (Endur and E/CTRM) raise project risk and cost

## Notes

Coverage gaps and caveats. (1) No mainframe or IBM i grain accounting system was confirmed. The legacy core in this category is pre-2005 on-prem PC and Windows client-server software (AGRIS, Agvance, AgTrax) plus 1990s-era CTRMs (TriplePoint 1993, Openlink). S1 is met on client-server only. (2) S4 rests on recruiter and consultancy claims about Endur/CTRM skills, mostly energy. There is no COBOL or RPG evidence, so treat it as weak. (3) Several primary pages returned 403 or were unreadable: NFA Philippines, AIC parliamentary evidence, the USWA FY2024 annual report (grain licence counts are in chart images), the CTRM Center vendor profiles, the Bunge and Cargill Covantis releases, and the Eka Medium post. Figures from them are excluded or marked unverified. (4) Buyer-universe counts mix different units (facilities, trade-association members, licences, clearances), so they are not comparable across regions. Thailand has no verified count. The Singapore GTP figure covers all commodities and dates from 2018. India's e-NAM count is mostly small mandi traders. (5) The Eka spreadsheet survey page shows a 2025 date on Quoreka's republished site, but the survey may be older. The sample was 50 executives across ag, chemicals and energy. (6) No AI-native CTRM challenger with verified funding was found on a primary page. Secondary results mention CommodityAI (about $800K seed, 2024) and Sparta (€40M Series B, energy-led), neither verified, so both are excluded. (7) ADM 10-K quotes were pulled from SEC EDGAR HTML via curl.
